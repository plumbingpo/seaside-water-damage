Few emergencies in a building are as hazardous as sewage backing up into it. Pathogens in raw sewage include salmonella, giardia, hepatitis and E. coli, along with other viruses, parasites and bacteria, and contact with anything contaminated is a serious risk to health. Seaside Restoration provides emergency sewage cleanup throughout Seaside, responding 24/7 with properly trained technicians, appropriate personal protective equipment and EPA-registered disinfectants to decontaminate your property safely.

Sewage damage is classified as Category 3 (black water), the most contaminated and dangerous category of water damage. Because of it, cleanup follows the toughest rules in the trade, which means full protective gear, barriers to contain the area, tearing out any porous material that met sewage and disinfecting all that is left. We work to the IICRC S500 standard that governs Category 3 losses, with the goal of returning your property to a condition safe to occupy.

We clear the standing sewage first, then pull contaminated water out of structural materials, remove carpet, padding, insulation, drywall and any other porous item that was in contact with sewage for proper disposal, disinfect each hard surface, treat with antimicrobial products and finish with structural drying, which prevents secondary mold. Lingering sewage odor is eliminated with thermal fogging and industrial hydroxyl generators, which we run in the affected area.

Signs It Is Time for Sewage Cleanup

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • A cleanout that weeps sewage, or wet foul patches in the lawn along the sewer path
  • Gurgling sounds from toilets or drains when other fixtures are used
  • Unexplained illness or gastrointestinal symptoms among household members
  • Toilet water with waste in it soaking into carpet or running across the floor
  • Dark, dirty water or sewage rising out of a tub, toilet or floor drain
  • Multiple drains in your home backing up at the same time
  • Every sink, tub and shower draining slowly even after you plunge them

Why Sewage Cleanup Is Not a DIY Job

No homeowner should try to clean up sewage personally. Because raw sewage is a biological hazard, the work demands trained people, protective equipment (gloves, boots, respirators and Tyvek suits) and disinfection products registered with the EPA and not sold to the public. Hepatitis, gastrointestinal disease and infections of the skin or respiratory system are among the serious illnesses these pathogens cause.

Disposal matters as well, since everything the sewage contaminated has to be handled as biohazard waste. Simply placing sewage-contaminated drywall, carpet and insulation in your regular trash is both illegal and hazardous to sanitation workers and the community. A certified restoration company follows proper disposal protocols and works with licensed facilities that accept biohazard waste.

Maintenance Tips for Sewage Cleanup

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • A drain that is slowing down should be dealt with early, while it is still partly open
  • Schedule a camera inspection of the sewer line every 2 to 3 years so trouble is found early
  • Wipes clog sewer lines even when the label says flushable, so only toilet paper goes down the toilet
  • Protect against municipal sewer backups by having a backwater valve fitted where your line connects
  • Keep cooking oil, grease and food scraps out of the drain, where they build into clogs
  • Keep downspouts and sump pump discharge out of the sanitary sewer so it does not overload in heavy rain
