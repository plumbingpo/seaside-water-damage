A dry house is not yet a finished house. After readings return to normal, the bare subfloor, cut-out drywall and missing floors still need attention. Water damage repair is the rebuild. In Seaside, we put back the materials that were lost and match them to the originals, turning a work zone into a normal room again.

No two repair jobs are the same size. On a small job it may be a few feet of drywall, new baseboard and paint. Large jobs can include insulation, whole drywall sheets, subfloor, laminate or hardwood, cabinet boxes, doors and trim. We replace materials like for like where possible, and we tell you plainly when an exact match is not available so you can choose.

We do not close anything in while it is still damp. Before any new material goes on, we confirm with a moisture meter that framing and subfloor are at normal levels. Mold hidden in a newly painted wall usually traces back to someone covering damp lumber. You also receive a written scope and schedule ahead of the work, which spells out what gets rebuilt and when.

How Our Water Damage Repair Works

We follow a proven process to ensure every job is completed to the highest standards. Here's what you can expect when you choose Seaside Restoration:

1

Confirm It Is Dry

Our crew meters the remaining drywall, the subfloor and the framing one last time. Until the readings are normal for every material, no reconstruction happens, and no moisture gets trapped.

The Benefits of Professional Water Damage Repair

This is not the same as a standard renovation project. A rebuilder must know the path the water took, the harm it caused and if the structure hidden by the wall is actually dry. We build from the moisture records made during drying, which tell us the studs that got wet, the subfloor that was treated and the spots needing a second look. That history keeps problems from being covered over with new drywall and fresh paint.

The second skill is matching. A patch that shows is a repair you notice every day. When a match is not possible, we lay out options, such as replacing flooring to a doorway. Our team pulls any permits the job needs and sends updates to your insurance company.

Maintenance Tips for Water Damage Repair

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Fresh caulk every year around showers, tubs and sinks stops water reaching the wall cavity.
  • Completed-repair photos and the scope document belong in your home records.
  • Fix the source first, and only then patch or paint a water stain.
  • Laundry rooms, bathrooms and kitchens should be rebuilt with moisture resistant wallboard and proper backer board.
  • Take pictures of damaged materials prior to removal, since the insurer may want them later.
  • Save spare tile and flooring, plus paint color details, for matching later repairs.

Warning Signs for Water Damage Repair

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Paint is bubbling, peeling or stained brown where water ran down the wall.
  • Trim around doors and along the floor is bowed and will not lie flat.
  • Seams are opening, or edges are curling and swelling, on vinyl plank or laminate.
  • A leak overhead left the ceiling drywall cracked, sagging or falling apart.
  • Flooring flexes more than it should in the spot that was flooded.
  • Wall cavities remain open where drywall was removed for drying.
  • Water under the tile left cracked grout lines or tiles that rock when stepped on.
