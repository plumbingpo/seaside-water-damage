Sump Pump Failure Cleanup Help in Seaside

A sump pump has one job, and it tends to fail at the moment it is needed most. Heavy rain raises the water table, the pit fills, and then the power goes out, the float sticks or the pump cannot keep up. Water rises over the rim of the pit and spreads across the basement floor. It keeps coming until a pump is running again.

This water is not clean. Having passed through the ground, it usually holds bacteria, silt and drain tile residue, which makes it Category 2 at best, or Category 3 once it has been standing or sewage has mixed in. For the job, this means soaked porous items such as drywall and carpet pad are usually discarded, and what stays gets washed and disinfected first, then dried.

Do This First

Your First Steps

  1. Stay Out of the WaterWith the electricity still on, a flooded basement is not safe to walk into. The furnace, wall outlets or the cord of the pump itself could be in contact with it.
  2. Turn Off the Basement CircuitsBasement breakers should be flipped off, provided you can get to the panel without standing in water. If the panel is in the water, call the utility.
  3. Check the Pump SafelyLook to see whether the pump came unplugged, its outlet tripped or the float is pinned against the pit wall. Do not reach into the water.
  4. Assume the Water Is DirtyIf going down cannot be avoided, put on gloves and rubber boots, leave pets and children upstairs, and wash up after handling wet items.
  5. Move Valuables and CallCarry dry valuables upstairs, photograph the water level and the pump, then call us. Holding off until the basement empties is not the answer.
What We Do

Our Sump Pump Failure Cleanup Process

1

Pump Out the Basement

Because the pumps and extraction units are ours, the broken sump does not hold up the work. We clear silt and standing water off the floor, out of low areas and from around the pit.

2

Take Out What Got Wet

Wearing protective gear, we take out the carpet, pad, wet drywall and insulation that the groundwater touched, bag it and haul it away so clean framing and concrete are exposed.

3

Wash and Disinfect

The slab, wall framing and remaining contents are washed down and treated with an antimicrobial product. Doing it ahead of drying keeps the air movers from spreading contaminants through the basement.

4

Dry and Verify

We run drying equipment until our meters show normal moisture in the slab and the wood, which typically takes 3 to 5 days. Before we pull our equipment, the pit should have a pump that works.

Insurance

What About Insurance?

Sump pump losses are widely misunderstood when it comes to insurance. Standard homeowners coverage generally leaves out sump overflow and backup, while flood coverage is bought separately and follows other rules. Unless you bought an endorsement for sump overflow or water backup there is often no coverage, and such endorsements are capped. Check the declarations page for it, or call your agent to find out. We record the pump failure, the water level and the damage so your claim file is complete.

Why It Happens

Why This Happens

  • Dirt and small rocks washed into the basin that lock up the pump.
  • Outside the house, a crushed, clogged or separated discharge pipe.
  • Check valve trouble, which makes the pump move the same water again and again.
  • A pump too small for the volume of water entering the pit.
  • Years of on and off cycling finally burning out an aging motor.
If It Is Left

What Happens if You Wait

Wall Framing

Bottom plates rest directly on concrete, so they are the first framing to get wet. We clean, disinfect and dry the studs and plates after removing the wet drywall.

Basement Floor Coverings

Carpet and pad soaked by groundwater cannot be cleaned to a safe standard and are removed. Vinyl plank and laminate floors generally have to be pulled as well, since dirty water stays under them.

Low Electrical Outlets

Rising water can get into receptacles mounted low on basement walls. Before those circuits are used again, an electrician needs to inspect whatever was under water.

Boxes and Stored Items

Books, cloth, boxes and wood furniture legs take in dirty water within a short time. Within days mold shows up on absorbent things that were in it, and they are difficult to disinfect.
