First Five Minutes

What to Do Right Now

  1. Cut the Power or GasElectric units get shut down at the breaker. For gas, turn the control knob to off. Never leave a heating element or burner running on an empty tank.
  2. Shut the Cold Water ValveThe cold line over the tank has a valve, and it closes with a clockwise turn. If it is stuck or leaking, shut off the main water valve for the house.
  3. Watch for Hot WaterWhat comes out of the tank can cause scalds. Let pooled water cool off before anyone steps in it, and keep kids and pets out of the area.
  4. Empty the Tank OutsideIf you can reach the drain valve, attach a garden hose and run it outside or to a floor drain so the remaining water does not empty onto the floor.
  5. Clear the FloorAnything absorbent, including boxes and rugs, should come up from the wet area. Because cardboard and fabric take in water quickly, they hold dampness against the walls and the slab.

Our Water Heater Leak Cleanup Process

1

Extract the Water

Coverage

What About Insurance?

Water damage to belongings, walls and floors from a tank that fails suddenly is often covered as a sudden and accidental loss. Replacing the heater is usually not included, because a tank wearing out from age counts as maintenance. Rust trails, old staining or other evidence that the leak was long-term can lead an adjuster to reduce or deny the claim, since gradual leaks usually fall outside coverage. Until the claim is closed, hold on to the old heater. Photograph it along with the damage, and review your policy.

Common Causes

Why This Happens

  • Loose plumbing connections left after a recent installation or repair.
  • A blocked or rusted drain pan under an attic or closet heater that overflows instead of draining.
  • An aging steel tank whose bottom is eaten through by corrosion and sediment.
  • Anode rods that are used up, leaving the steel inside the tank open to rust.
  • The relief valve for temperature and pressure opening, or failing, and letting water out.
If It Is Left

What the Water Damages

Items Stored Near the Heater

The first casualties are usually the furniture, boxes and keepsakes stored around the heater in a garage or closet. Cardboard falls apart, and wet contents can mold if they are not dried or discarded quickly.

Attic and Ceilings

A heater in the attic sends water through insulation onto the ceiling below. Insulation that is wet no longer insulates, and the added weight can pull the ceiling drywall down.

Garage Wall Base

As water runs across the garage slab, it wets the drywall and bottom plate of the wall the garage shares with the house and continues into flooring on the other side.

Closet Walls

Drywall surrounding a heater closet inside the house wicks water up and lets it through to the neighboring rooms. Swollen baseboards and a wall bottom that is still damp within are typical.
