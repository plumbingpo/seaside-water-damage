No part of restoring a water-damaged building matters more than structural drying, yet it is also where untrained crews make the most mistakes. Extraction takes care of standing water, but drywall, framing, subflooring, insulation, concrete and other building materials stay loaded with trapped moisture. A structure left wet ends up with odors, wood rot, mold and failing materials, so the moisture has to be removed in a scientific way using dehumidification and controlled evaporation.

The structural drying technicians at Seaside Restoration are IICRC-certified and trained in psychrometry, the science of how temperature, humidity and moisture in air relate. Using this knowledge, they calculate the specific equipment placement, temperature settings and airflow patterns needed to create the right evaporation conditions for each drying situation. Different materials (drywall, hardwood, concrete, plaster) dry at different rates and need different approaches to reach acceptable moisture levels without causing additional damage.

Our structural drying process includes daily moisture monitoring with calibrated moisture meters, thermo-hygrometers and thermal imaging cameras. We document moisture readings throughout the drying process, creating a detailed drying log that shows progressive moisture reduction in all affected materials. Equipment is adjusted daily based on these readings to optimize drying efficiency. Drying is complete only when all affected materials reach acceptable moisture standards as defined by IICRC S500 guidelines, not when surfaces simply feel dry to the touch.

Maintenance Tips for Structural Drying

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Go by the meter, not by touch, since a dry-feeling surface may still hold moisture deep inside
  • Do not place personal items on top of drying equipment or block the airflow from air movers
  • Leave the drying equipment on even if the noise bothers you, since each hour counts
  • The dehumidifiers and air movers should run around the clock until our technician takes them out
  • Tell your restoration technician immediately if you notice new wet areas or odors during drying
  • Leave equipment where the technician placed it, since positions are based on the moisture map

What a Professional Brings to Structural Drying

There is real science behind structural drying, which is why guessing does not work. Secondary mold damage most often traces back to someone who put down fans and hoped things would dry. To do it professionally, you have to know how each material takes on and gives up moisture, work out the grain depression, meaning the moisture removal capacity, for the job, and check progress every day. Lacking that understanding, people either stop too soon and leave moisture for mold, or dry too hard and harm wood and other materials.

Professional drying equipment is not a bigger version of what stores sell, it is a different kind of machine. A commercial LGR dehumidifier, short for low-grain refrigerant, pulls 10 to 20 times as much moisture out of the air as a residential one, while high-velocity air movers set up the exact airflow that speeds evaporation off structural surfaces. This equipment represents a significant investment and requires training to deploy effectively, both reasons why professional drying produces far better outcomes than DIY attempts.

How to Tell You Need Structural Drying

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Wood flooring with boards that cup, crown or pull apart
  • Moisture meter readings above normal in walls, floors or ceilings after water damage
  • Wall finishes that start to peel or bubble in the days after water damage
  • Trapped moisture often announces itself with a persistent musty odor after cleanup
  • Wallboard with water stains, puffiness or a spongy feel
  • No visible water left, yet walls or floors still feel cold and damp
  • Baseboards or door trim that swell, warp or pull away from the wall
