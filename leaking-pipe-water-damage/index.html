Leaking Pipe Water Damage Help in Seaside

You seldom catch a pipe leak the day it begins. Water seeps out behind drywall, at the back of a sink cabinet or under the concrete until a stain, a soft floor or an unexplained water bill gives it away. Often that means weeks of moisture have already gone into whatever surrounds the pipe. We serve Seaside homeowners by tracing the full wet area and drying every bit of it, including what is out of sight.

The plumbing repair stops the water, but it does not remove what has already soaked in. Getting it out is the work we do. We use moisture meters and thermal imaging to map the wet materials, open only what has to be opened, and set air movers and dehumidifiers until readings are back to normal. Because slow leaks give mold time to start, we also check for growth and treat affected surfaces.

First Five Minutes

Your First Steps

  1. Shut Off the WaterWhen a toilet or sink line is dripping, shut the valve on the supply right at that fixture. When the source is unclear, the house main is the valve to turn off.
  2. Stay Clear of ElectricityWater around a switch, outlet or light fixture means the breaker for that part of the house should go off. Do not touch wet electrical devices or cords.
  3. Check the Water MeterMake sure nothing in the house is using water and read the meter. A dial that keeps turning points to a hidden pipe leak, which may be under the slab.
  4. Empty and PhotographClear out the cabinet or closet near the leak and take photos of stains, warped materials and the pipe itself before anything is repaired or thrown away.
  5. Have the Area MeteredAsk for meter readings of the area before anyone patches it closed. Drywall can feel dry to the touch while the cavity behind it is still damp.
Insurance

Insurance and Leaking Pipe Water Damage

Insurers draw a line between a sudden leak and a slow one. Coverage is common for accidental, sudden damage, while a leak that ran for months or weeks is usually not covered and is specifically excluded in some policies. If the leak was concealed and you could not have known, it may be viewed more favorably than a visible leak nobody dealt with. Read your policy and talk to your agent. So the adjuster can work from solid facts, we provide photos, moisture readings and notes on the leak source.

Why It Happens

What Causes It

  • Water pressure set too high, which wears on every joint and valve until a slow weep begins.
  • Fasteners from remodeling work or wall hangings that nick or pierce a line inside the wall.
  • Older copper pipe that corrodes from the inside until a pinhole opens in the wall of the line.
  • Hidden joints, soldered or crimped, that fail behind walls and above ceilings.
  • A compression fitting or supply line connection at a sink or toilet that has loosened or worn with use.
If It Is Left

What Happens if You Wait

Flooring Damage

A leak in a wall or under the slab sends water beneath the floor covering. Hardwood cups, laminate swells at the seams, and adhesive under vinyl and tile lets go.

Hidden Mold Growth

Nothing suits mold better than the dark, moist space inside a wall. It can spread across the hidden face of the drywall long before anything appears on the room side, which is common when a leak is slow.

Ceiling and Drywall Damage

A pipe leaking above a ceiling or inside a wall leaves brown rings and bubbled paint. Drywall that stays wet goes soft, sags and may need to be cut out.

Ruined Cabinet Bases

Particleboard cabinet bases under a sink swell and crumble once they absorb water. Because the board never shrinks back, a long leak often ends with a new cabinet base.
