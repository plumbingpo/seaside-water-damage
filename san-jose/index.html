How San Jose Homeowners Can Avoid Water Damage

Most of the water damage we see in San Jose homes started with something small that gave warning first. A few minutes of checking a couple of times a year catches most of it.

Begin at the hoses. Washer, dishwasher, toilet and refrigerator lines made of plain rubber age badly, and swapping them for braided steel is a cheap fix. Look under every sink for stains, swollen cabinet floors or a musty smell. Check the water heater for rust at the base and drain a little from the tank once a year to clear sediment. Restoration Challenges San Jose Homeowners Face

Walk through a house from the roof down and you pass most of the places a leak can begin. Our crews in San Jose follow that same path on an inspection, and these five stops cause the most trouble.

Attics: The top of the house takes on water when shingles are damaged or the seal around a vent pipe gives out. Insulation acts like a sponge, so it can hold a surprising amount before any of it shows on the ceiling. Once soaked, that material no longer does its job and holds moisture against the ceiling joists. We take out the ruined material, dry the framing and leave the roof repair itself to a roofer.

Bathrooms: Bathroom floods usually come from a forgotten bathtub, a stopped-up sink or a toilet that will not quit rising. Tile looks waterproof, yet water gets through grout lines and around the edges of the room. When the bathroom is on an upper story, the water shows up as a wet patch overhead downstairs. Anything that came out of the toilet bowl should be treated as dirty water and cleaned with disinfectant.

Walls: Supply lines and drain pipes run inside the walls, and a slow leak there can go on for a long time. You might see trim pulling away, a discolored spot spreading or drywall that gives when pressed. On these calls in San Jose, an infrared camera and a pin meter show us how far the dampness reaches. After the pipe is repaired by a plumber, we open just the damp part and dry the cavity.

Tanks: A water heater holds a large volume under pressure, usually in a closet, garage or utility room. Drips from the relief valve, the drain valve or the fittings on top are early signs that parts are wearing. When the tank itself fails, close the cold water valve above it and switch off the gas or power to the unit. Our Seaside Restoration crew extracts the water and checks the nearby walls and flooring, which often get wet from behind.

Drains: The lowest drain in the house is where a sewer blockage shows itself. If murky water comes up in a downstairs shower or floor drain, waste has nowhere to go but back inside. It falls under Category 3, meaning heavily contaminated, so cleanup calls for proper protective gear. Hold off on flushing or running taps, and keep the household clear while our team heads to San Jose.
